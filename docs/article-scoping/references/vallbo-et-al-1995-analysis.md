# Vallbo et al. 1995 — Detailed Analysis

**Full title:** Receptive field characteristics of tactile units with
myelinated afferents in hairy skin of human subjects

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Vallbo, Olausson, Wessberg, Kakuda |
| **Journal** | The Journal of Physiology 483(3):783-795 |
| **Species / site** | Human, hairy forearm (lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve) |
| **PDF** | `vallbo-et-al-1995-hairy-skin-ab-mechanoreceptors.pdf` |

---

## Sample Size

55 low-threshold mechanoreceptive units total, from 21 subjects (9 females,
12 males, aged 21-31).

| Type | n | % of total |
|------|---|------------|
| SAI (Merkel) | 21 | 38 |
| SAII (Ruffini) | 15 | 27 |
| Hair | 12 | 22 |
| Field | 5 | 9 |
| Pacinian (PC) | 2 | 4 |

Of these, 35 units were mapped in detail for receptive field structure.
No PC fields were explored in detail. SAII count may be inflated because
spontaneous firing made them easier for the experimenter to notice.

---

## Stimulus Properties

### Primary stimulus: Automated scanning probe (RF mapping)

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Stimulus type | Moving mechanical indentation (scanning probe) |
| Probe geometry | Metal tube with half-sphere tip, 1 mm diameter |
| Indentation force | Light, approximately constant (probe weight 0.22-0.25 g, held perpendicular by gravity through plastic bushes) |
| Probe speed | 10 mm/s (standard); 18, 30, or 33 mm/s for 8 units |
| Scan direction | Proximodistal along forearm; each track traversed twice (distal-to-proximal then proximal-to-distal) |
| Track spacing | 0.23 mm (parallel tracks) |
| Plotter step size | 0.032 mm |
| Scan area | 15 x 15 mm rectangular (standard); larger for hair/field units |
| Delivery | Modified X-Y plotter, microcomputer-controlled |
| Skin preparation | Shaved (hair cut short) |

### Secondary stimuli (unit identification / threshold)

| Stimulus | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Calibrated von Frey hairs (0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1.3, 2.5, 5.0, 10, 20, 40, 80 mN) | Threshold measurement and general exploration |
| Strain-gauge device | Manual exploration of afferent properties |
| Sustained skin indentation | Classify slowly vs. rapidly adapting |
| Hair movement / light air puffs | Identify hair units |
| Remote taps | Identify Pacinian (PC) units and distinguish field units |

---

## Unit Identification Criteria

| Type | Criteria |
|------|----------|
| SAI | Slowly adapting to sustained indentation; skewed interspike interval histogram; CV of interspike intervals 0.38-1.05 (median, range; n=12); no spontaneous firing |
| SAII | Slowly adapting; symmetrical interspike interval histogram; CV of interspike intervals 0.04-0.31 (n=11); 8/10 fired spontaneously |
| Hair | Rapidly adapting; brisk response to movement of individual hairs and light air puffs |
| Field | Rapidly adapting; multiple high-sensitivity spots; not particularly sensitive to hair movements; no response to remote taps |
| Pacinian | Rapidly adapting; single spot of maximal sensitivity; vigorous response to remote taps on large forearm areas |

## Receptive Field Properties

### RF areas (from Table 1)

| Type | n (area) | Median area (mm^2) | Range (mm^2) | Median threshold (mN) | n (threshold) |
|------|----------|--------------------|--------------|-----------------------|---------------|
| SAI | 11 | 11 | 3.7-21 | 0.45 | 10 |
| SAII | 10 | 1.4 | 0.5-14 | 1.3 | 7 |
| Hair | 10 | 113 | 44-21 | -- | -- |
| Field | 4 | 78 | 42-115 | 0.10 | 3 |
| PC | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |

### SAI (Merkel) field structure

- 2-4 well-separated high-sensitivity spots per field
- Individual spot size: median 0.8 mm^2 (range 0.2-2.2 mm^2, n=21)
- Inter-spot distance: median 3.0 mm (centre-to-centre, minimal chain)
- Distance between peripheries of most separated spots: median 6.4 mm
- Often had an additional dwarf spot (5/11 fields), median 0.02 mm^2, located distal, proximal, medial, or within the main set
- Spot arrangements: triangle (n=6), quadrangle (n=1), straight line (n=2)
- No preferred orientation relative to arm axis
- None of 11 SAI units fired spontaneously
- Spots correspond to Merkel cell clusters / touch domes

### SAII (Ruffini) field structure

- 9/10 fields had a single responsive spot; only 1/10 had two spots
- 8/10 fired spontaneously at a low, regular rate
- Spontaneous discharge paused for a few tenths of a second when the probe passed over the high-sensitivity spot
- Median RF area: 1.4 mm^2 (defined as area where rate exceeded 3x spontaneous rate, or the entire responsive area for non-spontaneous units)

### Hair unit field structure

- An order of magnitude larger than SAI/SAII
- Median area: 113 mm^2; oval or irregular shape
- 10-33+ innervated hairs per unit (median long axis 16 mm, short axis 11 mm)
- Sensory endings fairly evenly but thinly distributed over the field
- Proximal probe movement (against hair direction) more effective than distal, even after shaving

### Field unit field structure

- Large fields (median 78 mm^2), similar range to hair units
- Oval or irregular, no orientation preference (median long axis 13 mm, short axis 7.0 mm)
- Numerous closely packed high-sensitivity spots with diffuse borders
- Conservative estimate: median 11 spots per unit (range 10-12, n=4)
- More confluent than hair unit maps (single confluent area vs. archipelago pattern)
- Extremely sensitive: median threshold 0.10 mN (5x lower than SAI, 3x lower than SAII)

## Stimulus-Dependent Observations

- **Force**: Light indentation revealed fine RF structure; stronger indentation blurred the maps (control experiments confirmed this). The uniform light force used was particularly small for SAII (relative to their thresholds) and high for field units, meaning SAII maps may underestimate and field unit maps may overestimate true structure.
- **Speed**: Higher probe speeds slightly reduced the apparent size of individual high-sensitivity spots; other field characteristics were not much affected.
- **Direction**: Hair units responded more strongly to proximal probe movement (against hair growth direction), even after shaving. Other unit types showed no consistent directional asymmetry.
- **RF is not invariant**: The receptive field's exact size, shape, and geography depend on stimulus characteristics; it is not a fixed entity (Johansson 1978).

## Regional Comparisons (from Discussion)

Forearm RA units (hair + field) have much larger RFs than equivalent units
at other body sites:

| Site | RA RF area (mm^2) | SAI RF area (mm^2) | Source |
|------|-------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Forearm (hairy) | 78-113 | 11 | This study |
| Hand dorsum (hairy) | 24 | 16 | Edin & Abbs 1991 |
| Face | 17 | 7 | Johansson et al. 1988 |
| Glabrous hand (FAI) | 13 | 11 | Johansson & Vallbo 1980 |

SAI field sizes are remarkably similar across all regions (~7-16 mm^2),
while rapidly adapting units vary by an order of magnitude.
