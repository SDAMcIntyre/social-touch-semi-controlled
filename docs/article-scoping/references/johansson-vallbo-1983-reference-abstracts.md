## 1. Békésy, von, G. V. (1967) *Sensory Inhibition*, Princeton University Press

**Status:** FOUND (book description)

Psychological experiments carried out over nearly forty years led Georg von
Békésy to realize that inhibition interconnects the fields of vision, hearing,
skin sensations, taste, and smell. This book creates the field of sensory
inhibition as a significant area for study, describing how excitation of sensory
neurons leads to reduction of activity in surrounding neurons, promoting contrast
enhancement. Based on the Herbert Sidney Langfeld memorial lectures (1965).

## 2. Burgess, P. R. & Perl, E. R. (1973) in *Handbook of Sensory Physiology*, Vol. 2, pp. 29–78

**Status:** FOUND (summary)

Reviews the properties and characteristics of cutaneous mechanoreceptors and
nociceptors. Explains how nonnoxious deformation of human skin with a small
stimulator evokes sensations referred to as "touch," which fade if the
stimulator remains stationary but can be distinguished even with rapidly repeated
stimuli when the stimulator moves perpendicular to the skin. Provides a
comprehensive overview of sensory responses and receptor mechanisms.

## 4. Hulliger, M., Nordh, E., Thelin, A.-E. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1979) *J. Physiol.* 291, 233–249

**Status:** FOUND

Examined how sensory nerve fibers in the hand's glabrous skin respond during
voluntary finger movements. Recorded activity from 111 single units in the
median nerve. All four types of mechanoreceptor units, and 77% of the single
units, were activated by isotonic movements, with Pacinian corpuscles showing the
strongest reactions. Almost all responsive units were excited during the dynamic
phase of ramp and smooth oscillatory movements. Concluded that dynamic responses
to voluntary movements matched responses to direct skin indentation.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/480210/

## 6. Johansson, R. S. (1978) *J. Physiol.* 281, 101–123

**Status:** FOUND

Nerve impulses in tactile units innervating the glabrous skin area of the human
hand were recorded from the median nerve. Four types were identified: two rapidly
adapting (RA and PC) and two slowly adapting (SA I and SA II). RA and SA I units
had receptive fields with several zones of maximal sensitivity over a
circular/oval area covering 5–10 papillary ridges. PC and SA II units had single
zones of peak sensitivity with gradually increasing threshold at the periphery.
SA II units were sensitive to the direction of lateral skin stretch. Results
indicate RA and SA I units suit high spatial resolution analysis, whereas PC and
SA II units suit vibration analysis and skin tension.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/702358/
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1282686/

## 7. Johansson, R. S. (1979) in *Sensory Functions of the Skin of Humans*, pp. 129–145

**Status:** FOUND (approximate)

Chapter titled "Tactile afferent units with small and well demarcated receptive
fields in the glabrous skin area of the human hand." Focuses on the
classification and properties of four types of tactile mechanoreceptive units
(Pacinian corpuscles, rapidly adapting, slowly adapting types I and II), their
detection thresholds, and relative densities in the hand. From the proceedings of
the Second International Symposium on Skin Senses (Florida State University,
1978).

## 8. Johansson, R. S. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1979) *J. Physiol.* 286, 283–300

**Status:** FOUND

Single unit impulses recorded with percutaneously inserted tungsten needle
electrodes from the median nerve. A sample of 334 low threshold mechanoreceptive
units innervating the glabrous skin were studied and separated into four groups
(RA, PC, SA I, SA II). Receptive field locations were determined and relative
unit densities within various skin regions calculated. Overall density increased
in the proximo-distal direction, with a slight increase from palm to main finger
and an abrupt increase from main finger to fingertip.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/439026/
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1281571/

## 9. Johansson, R. S. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1979) *J. Physiol.* 297, 405–422

**Status:** FOUND

Psychophysical thresholds were determined at 162 points in the glabrous skin
using slowly rising triangular indentations. The method of constant stimuli was
used with forced-choice or yes-no procedures. Thresholds from the volar aspect
of fingers and peripheral parts of the palm were low (unimodal, median 11.2 μm).
The centre of the palm, lateral aspects of fingers, and crease regions showed
higher thresholds (slightly bimodal distribution, median 36.0 μm).

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/536918/
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1458728/

## 10. Johansson, R. S. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1980) *Brain Res.* 184, 353–366

**Status:** FOUND

Impulses recorded from low threshold mechanoreceptive afferents in median nerve
of waking subjects. Four groups identified (RA, PC, SA I, SA II). Receptive
fields of 255 units were mapped with von Frey hairs. RA and SA I units had
similar-sized fields (medians 12.6 and 11.0 mm² respectively), while SA II and
PC units had fields about 5 and 10 times larger.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7353161/

## 12. Johansson, R. S., Landström, U. & Lundström, R. (1982) *Brain Res.* 244, 27–32

**Status:** FOUND

One type of rapidly adapting (RA) and one type of slowly adapting (SA I)
mechanoreceptive unit innervating the glabrous skin have small well-defined
receptive fields. Their responses to perpendicular sinusoidal skin displacements
were measured when the field was completely covered versus when the edge of a
contact surface was placed over the field. This edge sensitivity, greatest for
SA I units, may be particularly important for improving spatial analysis of
object edges during manual manipulation and exploration.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6288181/

## 15. Knibestöl, M. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1970) *Acta Physiol. Scand.* 80, 178–195

**Status:** FOUND

Single unit impulses recorded from median and ulnar nerves in waking human
subjects with percutaneously inserted tungsten electrodes. 61 mechanoreceptor
units in glabrous skin analysed. Four different types of receptors distinguished
based mainly on adaptation and receptive field characteristics. The fields had
very sharp borders for the majority of both slowly and rapidly adapting units.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5475340/

## 16. Knibestöl, M. & Vallbo, Å. B. (1980) *J. Physiol.* 300, 251–267

**Status:** FOUND

Impulses recorded from single afferent fibres in median and ulnar nerves.
Response of slowly adapting mechanosensitive units tested during rectangular
indentations of varying amplitudes and invariant duration. Simultaneously,
subjects estimated sensation magnitude. A relationship was found between
discharge frequency and subjective sensation magnitude. The correlation between
total afferent input (summed discharge frequency of all active units) and
subjective estimates was even closer, suggesting magnitude of tactile sensation
is primarily encoded by total activity in the slowly adapting population.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7381785/

## 17. Lemon, R. N. (1981) *J. Physiol.* 311, 497–519

**Status:** FOUND

Recordings from area 4 of the cerebral cortex in five conscious monkeys. Of 216
neurones responsive to hand/finger stimulation: 46% responded only to cutaneous
stimulation (especially light brushing across glabrous skin), 38% responded only
to digit movement, 4% to brief prods, 12% to more than one modality. Many
neurones responded at short latency (8–15 ms) to light mechanical stimulation
and weak electrical stimulation of the median nerve. Responsive neurones found at
all cortical depths, shortest latencies in layers IV and V.

- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1275426/

## 18. Marsden, C. D., Merton, P. A. & Morton, H. B. (1977) *J. Physiol.* 265, 521–535

**Status:** FOUND

Investigates sensory mechanisms underlying servo action in human muscles.
Anaesthesia of the thumb suppresses servo action in the long flexor during
terminal phalanx movements; greater subjective effort is required to flex an
anaesthetized thumb; anaesthesia of other digits has no effect on the thumb
flexor; giving the subject a visual tracking task can partially restore servo
action when the thumb is anaesthetic.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/850205/
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1307832/

## 24. Vallbo, Å. B. (1981) *Brain Res.* 215, 359–363

**Status:** FOUND

A needle microelectrode in the median nerve was used to record impulses from
tactile afferent units and to deliver trains of electrical pulses for
microstimulation. Activation of single SA I units produces a sensation of light
pressure within a small area located within the receptive field, whereas
activation of RA units elicited sensations of varying qualities: touch,
vibration, and tickle.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7260595/

---

## 25. Vallbo, Å. B. & Hagbarth, K.-E. (1968) *Exp. Neurol.* 21, 270–289

**Status:** FOUND

Describes a technique for recording multi-fiber discharge and single-unit
activity from intact peripheral nerves of awake human subjects. A tungsten
electrode (tip diameter 5–15 μ) was driven manually through skin, subcutaneous
tissues and nerve sheath. From many recording sites, neural impulses with
amplitude of 40 μV were recorded in response to peripheral mechanical stimuli.
Examples presented of fast and slowly adapting mass discharge induced by
mechanical stimuli on glabrous and non-glabrous skin areas.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5673644/
