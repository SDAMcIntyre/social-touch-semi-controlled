## 2. Quantitative Benchmarks — Myelinated RF Data on Hairy Skin

### Forearm (lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve)

1 mm diameter, 0.23 mm track spacing.

|----------|---|-------------------|-----------|-------|
| SA-I (Merkel) | 21 (38%) | 11 mm^2 | 2-4 well-separated spots (spot median 0.8 mm^2, inter-spot 3.0 mm) | Similar size across body regions |
| SA-II (Ruffini) | 15 (27%) | 1.4 mm^2 | 1 spot, diffuse borders | 8/10 spontaneously active |
| HFA (hair) | 12 (22%) | 113 mm^2 | 10-33+ innervated hairs, oval | Directional: proximal movement more effective |

**This remains the only primary RF size dataset for myelinated types on
the human forearm.** No other study has replicated or extended these
measurements at this body site.

## 4. Literature Gaps — What Remains Uncharacterised

### Confirmed gaps (no primary data exists)

1. **No RF mapping of myelinated types on the forearm beyond Vallbo
   1995.** Despite being the most-studied site for CT afferents, the
   forearm has exactly one primary dataset for Abeta RF sizes, from a
   single study using a scanning probe in 21 subjects.

2. **No naturalistic-stimulus RF mapping for any myelinated type on
   hairy skin.** All existing data use punctate probes (von Frey,
   scanning probes) or controlled sinusoidal stimulation. The response
   field under whole-hand social touch has never been mapped for
   myelinated afferents.

3. **No population-level RF tiling model for hairy skin.** Johansson &
   Vallbo 1980 built a sheath/overlap model for the glabrous hand; no
   equivalent exists for any hairy skin site.

4. **No systematic RF size comparison across hairy body sites.** Vallbo
   1995 (forearm), Edin 2001 (thigh), and Edin & Abbs 1991 (dorsal
   hand) each characterise one site, but no study compares RF properties
   across multiple hairy sites in the same subjects.

5. **SA-III characterisation limited to thigh.** Edin's SA-III class
   (2001, 2004) has not been confirmed at other hairy skin sites,
   including the forearm.

6. **Field unit data extremely sparse.** Only 5 units characterised on
   the forearm (Vallbo 1995) and 1 on the thigh (Edin 2001). This
   afferent type — the human homologue of the Abeta Field-LTMR
   extensively studied in mice (Bai et al. 2015) — is virtually
   uncharacterised in humans.

7. **No age-stratified RF data for hairy skin.** Corniani & Saal 2020
   estimate 5-8%/decade density decline but no study has measured how
   individual RF sizes change with age on hairy skin.

### Partially covered

- **HFA properties**: Well-characterised on forearm (Vallbo 1995) and
  thigh (Edin 2001), but no detailed subfield structure analysis
  comparable to the SA-I hotspot analysis in Vallbo 1995.
- **SA-II proprioceptive role**: Demonstrated on dorsal hand (Edin 1992,
  2004) and thigh (Edin 2001) but not on forearm specifically.
- **End-organ histology**: Ruffini corpuscle described on scalp (Halata
  1981), touch dome on general hairy skin (Reinisch 2005), but no
  combined electrophysiology + histology on the same site.

## 7. Conclusion — Expected Outcome Assessment

The investigation confirms **outcome (b) from the plan**: the literature
is genuinely as thin as it appeared.

For the **forearm** — the body site of the naturalistic study — Vallbo
et al. 1995 remains the sole primary RF dataset for myelinated
mechanoreceptors. No study has replicated, extended, or updated these
measurements in the 31 years since publication.

The search did uncover additional primary data at other hairy sites:
- Dorsal hand: Järvilehto 1976, Konietzny 1977, Edin 1991/1992/2004,
  Edin et al. 1995
- Thigh: Edin 2001

And important histological characterisations of end-organs:
- Ruffini in hairy skin: Halata & Munger 1981
- Touch dome / Merkel cells: Reinisch & Tschachler 2005

But the central finding is a **confirmed gap**: no RF mapping of
myelinated types on the forearm with naturalistic stimuli, no population
tiling model for hairy skin, and an extremely thin evidence base for
field units in humans. This strengthens the novelty argument for the
naturalistic RF study.
